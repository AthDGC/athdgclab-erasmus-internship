01 · The laboratory

A research home for language contact and change.

The AthDGC Laboratory investigates how languages influence each other across time and space. It combines historical philology, corpus methods and formal syntax, and it is hosted by the Department of English Language and Literature, Faculty of Philosophy, at the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens.

Our work spans several centuries and several traditions: from Byzantine and Early Modern Greek translations of biblical and classical texts, to syntactic change in the history of English, to bilingualism and language attrition in present-day communities.

The Laboratory works with international partners and welcomes Erasmus+ trainees into a close, supportive group with regular reading sessions and hands-on training in corpus annotation.

Research areas

  1. i.Diachronic syntax of Indo-European
  2. ii.Language contact and translation philology
  3. iii.Treebanking and corpus annotation
  4. iv.Bilingualism and heritage languages
  5. v.Computational philology and digital humanities
  6. vi.Voice, valency and argument structure
02 · The traineeship

What you'll actually do here.

A traineeship at the Laboratory is a real research apprenticeship. You join an active project, receive structured training, and contribute work that ends up in the Laboratory's outputs.

i.

Corpus and treebank work

Annotate historical Greek, Latin or English texts, and learn dependency annotation, part-of-speech tagging and digital edition workflows alongside experienced annotators.

ii.

A research project

Take on a focused mini-project, typically a case study in syntactic or lexical change, supervised by Professor Lavidas. The work can grow into a thesis chapter or a co-authored paper.

iii.

Laboratory life

Attend the Laboratory seminar, the History of Linguistics reading group and the visiting-speaker series. Present your work in progress and receive structured peer feedback.

Who can apply

Bachelor's students (advanced years), Master's students and doctoral candidates enrolled at a higher education institution that holds an Erasmus Charter for Higher Education. Backgrounds in linguistics, philology, classics, English or Greek studies, computer science and digital humanities are welcome. Recent graduates may also apply for a traineeship within twelve months of graduation, in line with Erasmus+ rules; ask your home Erasmus office how this works at your university.

03 · Reviewers

Review the work, in three strands.

The AthDGC Laboratory is building a corpus of historical Greek and other languages, annotated in the PROIEL scheme, and it is looking for people who review every part of that work. Trainees choose one strand or combine two. Reviewers from outside the laboratory are welcome too.

i.

Technical review

Checking the scripts and annotation pipelines, the file formats, data integrity, and the output of automatic annotation. Useful if you work with Python, XML or treebank data.

ii.

Linguistic review

Checking lemmas, morphology, syntax and case marking in the PROIEL treebanks against the annotation guidelines. Useful if you know PROIEL, or know Greek or another language of the corpus well enough to judge an analysis.

iii.

Philological review

Checking the texts themselves: the editions we use, OCR and transcription against their sources, and the period and provenance of each text, from Koine to early modern Greek. Useful if you work with manuscripts, editions or historical texts.

How to take part

Students apply as Erasmus+ trainees: write one PDF, say which strand you prefer, and follow the steps under How to apply. Reviewers who are not applying as trainees write to nlavidas@enl.uoa.gr, say which strand and which language, text or period they know best, and say how much time they could give.

04 · Funding

Who pays? Your home university.

The Erasmus+ traineeship grant is paid by your home university, not by the AthDGC Laboratory. The Laboratory doesn't pay a salary or a stipend.

Who pays the grant Your home university, from its own Erasmus+ funds. Your Erasmus office decides whether you are selected for a grant.
What the Laboratory provides A place in a research group, supervision, training, and a signed Learning Agreement for Traineeships. We also help with documentation, accommodation suggestions and a welcome. No salary and no additional stipend.
How much you receive The monthly amount and any supplements depend on the Erasmus+ programme rules and on your home university. Ask your home Erasmus office for the figure that applies to your placement. The official grant levels are published in the Erasmus+ Programme Guide.
Insurance The University of Athens provides no grant and no insurance. You need health insurance (your European Health Insurance Card or a private policy) and an insurance for personal accident and civil liability, in English, for the whole stay. Ask your home university whether it provides them.
Before you write to us Check with your home Erasmus office that you're eligible for a traineeship abroad and that it will fund your placement. Then send us your PDF.

06 · How to apply

Apply by email.

The call is open now. For traineeships starting from 7 January 2027, send your PDF by 15 December 2026; applications for later terms are read as they arrive. Applying is simple: send one PDF by email. The offer is also published on the Erasmus Careers platform.

  1. Before you apply

    Talk to your home Erasmus+ office

    Confirm that you're eligible for an Erasmus+ traineeship and that your university supports the placement. Ask about start dates, durations and the grant amount you would receive.

  2. Write one PDF

    Tell us about yourself

    Write a single PDF with your studies and background, your ideas, and what you'd like to do in the Laboratory, and say which strand and which period you'd prefer, for two to six months. No form and no separate documents are needed.

  3. Send it

    Email the PDF to nlavidas@enl.uoa.gr

    Send it by 15 December 2026 for traineeships starting from 7 January 2027. Applications for later terms are read as they arrive.

  4. We reply

    Within 20 days, by email

    We reply by email within 20 days of receiving your PDF, and we agree with you the practical timing of the placement, in line with your interests and the Laboratory's projects. The University's own processing of the formal application follows and can take longer.

  5. The University's formal steps

    Complete the University's online application

    Once we accept you, the European and International Relations Department of the University of Athens opens its own online application. You upload a Learning Agreement for Traineeships signed by you, your home university and the Laboratory's academic coordinator, your passport or identity card, your health insurance, and accident and civil-liability insurance in English for the whole stay. Your placement is confirmed only when you receive the Letter of Acceptance from that department, and your home university finalises the grant. In our reply we tell you exactly what to send and where.
